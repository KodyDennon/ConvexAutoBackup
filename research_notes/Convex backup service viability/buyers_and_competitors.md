# Who pays for BaaS and small-database backups, and what competitors charge

Observation date for pages fetched in this pass: 8 October 2026, unless a source prints its own “current as of” date. Amounts are USD unless the source states another currency. No market-size total is estimated below. Public deployment counts for Convex were not found.

## What do Supabase, Firebase/Google, Neon, PlanetScale, Xata, Turso, and Convex charge for backups, PITR, or retention, and which plans include them?

### Takeaway
Scheduled backups are usually bundled into a paid platform plan at no separate backup SKU, while point-in-time recovery is either a large add-on (Supabase, about $100 per 7 days), usage-priced history (Neon, Firestore), or included and lengthened as the plan gets more expensive (Turso, PlanetScale within a short default window). Convex already sells daily logical backups on Professional at $25 per developer per month, keeps them only 7 days (14 days if weekly), and has no point-in-time recovery.

### Cited Findings
- Supabase Free is $0/month and does not include automatic backups. Pro is from $25/month and includes daily backups stored for 7 days. Team is from $599/month and includes daily backups stored for 14 days. Enterprise is custom, with automatic backups listed as custom. Point-in-time recovery is an add-on at $100 per month per 7 days of retention on Pro, Team, and Enterprise; Enterprise can go beyond 28 days. Pro and Team include $10/month of compute credits, which covers one Micro compute instance at $10/month. Observed 8 October 2026. — [Supabase Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)
- Supabase backup docs: Pro projects can access the last 7 days of daily backups, Team 14 days, Enterprise up to 30 days. Free-tier projects are told to export with the CLI `db dump` command and keep off-site backups. PITR is an add-on for Pro, Team, and Enterprise, and those projects must also use at least a Small compute add-on. Enabling PITR stops daily backups. Published PITR rates: 7 days at $0.137/hour (about $100/month), 14 days at $0.274/hour (about $200/month), 28 days at $0.55/hour (about $400/month). Their worked example for one project for a full month is Pro $25 + Small compute $15 + 7-day PITR $100 − $10 compute credit = $130. PITR is not covered by the spend cap and is billed for each partial hour as a full hour. — [Supabase backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups); [Manage Point-in-Time Recovery usage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/point-in-time-recovery)
- Firebase / Cloud Firestore scheduled backups require the Blaze plan. Retention on a schedule can be set up to 14 weeks. Charges are for backup storage and, on restore, for the size of the backup. The Google Cloud Firestore pricing table lists backup data at $0.000041096 per GiB and restore operations at $0.20 per GiB, with free-quota column “Not supported” for backup data, PITR data, and restore. The same table lists PITR data at $0.000205479 per GiB, equal to the stored-data rate. A separate Firebase Enterprise edition pricing page lists backup data at $0.00004 per GiB-hour, PITR data at $0.00020 per GiB-hour, and restore at $0.20 per GiB. The Cloud pricing page’s prose says backup storage costs are calculated in GiB-months from how long each backup is retained. — [Firestore backups](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/backups); [Cloud Firestore pricing](https://cloud.google.com/firestore/pricing); [Firestore Enterprise pricing](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/enterprise/pricing)
- Neon has no separate backup plan fee and no minimum monthly fee on Launch or Scale. Instant restore (their point-in-time restore) history window: Free is 6 hours, capped at 1 GB of change history, no charge; Launch is up to 7 days at $0.20/GB-month; Scale is up to 30 days at $0.20/GB-month. Defaults are 6 hours on Free and 1 day on Launch and Scale. Only root-branch change history is billed. Ordinary storage is separate: 1 GB per project on Free, then $0.35/GB-month on Launch and Scale. Compute is 100 CU-hours per project on Free, then $0.106/CU-hour on Launch and $0.222/CU-hour on Scale. Manual snapshots: 1 on Free, 100 on Launch and Scale. — [Neon plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans); [History window](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/history-window)
- PlanetScale Postgres includes automatic backups every 12 hours on production and development branches, with no extra charge for those default backups. Each branch includes backup storage equal to 2× disk size (examples: 25 GB disk includes 50 GB backup storage; 50 GB disk includes 100 GB). Backup and WAL storage above that allowance is $0.023 per GB per month, billed on compressed size. A 100 GB overage for 30 days is shown as $2.30. Point-in-time recovery is available inside the retention window, by default from 2 days ago up to 5 minutes before the current time; custom schedules, manual backups, or preventing backup deletion extend the window and can add charges. Single-node PS-5 (1/16 vCPU, 512 MB, AWS us-east-1, x86 or ARM) is $5/month; highly available PS-5 is $15/month. Network-attached clusters include 10 GB of disk; storage beyond that is billed separately and was not copied into a single regional rate here. — [PlanetScale Postgres pricing](https://planetscale.com/docs/postgres/pricing); [PlanetScale Postgres backups](https://planetscale.com/docs/postgres/backups); [PlanetScale point-in-time recovery](https://planetscale.com/docs/postgres/backups/point-in-time-recovery)
- Turso includes point-in-time restore on every plan, with longer windows on higher plans, and states that point-in-time restore does not add a usage charge beyond consuming a database slot and storage quota. Free is $0: 100 databases, 5 GB storage, 1 day point-in-time restore, no restore of deleted databases. Developer is $5.99/month or $4.99/month billed yearly: 10 days point-in-time restore, 9 GB included storage then $0.75/GB. Scaler is $29/month or $24.92/month billed yearly: 30 days, 24 GB then $0.50/GB, teams included. Pro is $499/month or $416.58/month billed yearly: 90 days, 50 GB then $0.45/GB, SSO, BYOK, HIPAA, SOC2. Enterprise is custom, with BYOC and 24×7 support; the published feature list still shows 90 days of point-in-time restore. — [Turso pricing](https://turso.tech/pricing.md)
- Xata’s current pricing specification (Open Source / Xata Cloud / BYOC) does not list a backup or PITR line item. Xata Cloud is usage-based: the summary states $0.012/hour compute plus $0.28/GB/month storage, billed by the minute, after a 14-day trial. The marketing homepage says “PITR backups” and “Restore your database to any point in time” without a price or retention window. An older docs appendix in the `xataio/mdx-docs` repository describes a different plan set (Free / Pro / Enterprise): daily backups on all plans, free-plan users limited to one support-assisted daily-backup restoration per month, and Pro users getting unlimited support-assisted PITR requests. That appendix conflicts with the current usage-based plan names, so it is not treated as the October 2026 price. — [Xata pricing specification](https://xata.io/pricing.md); [Xata pricing page](https://xata.io/pricing/); [Xata homepage](https://xata.io/); [Older Xata pricing appendix](https://github.com/xataio/mdx-docs/blob/main/100-Appendix/020-pricing.mdx)
- Convex Free & Starter is $0 plus pay-as-you-go. The pricing page lists daily backups as not included. Professional is $25 per developer per month (1–20 developers) and lists daily backups as included, along with HIPAA/SOC 2 reports. Business & Enterprise is a $2,500 monthly minimum and lists fast backup/restore, service SLAs, and dedicated deployments. Deployment caps on the same page: 40 on Free & Starter, 300 on Professional, unlimited on Business & Enterprise. Footer is ©2026 Convex, Inc. Observed 8 October 2026. — [Convex pricing](https://www.convex.dev/pricing)
- Convex backup docs: a backup is a consistent snapshot of table data, optionally including file storage, and does not include code, environment variables, or scheduled functions. Manual backups are stored for 7 days. Periodic daily backups are stored for 7 days; weekly backups for 14 days. Periodic backups require Convex Pro. Free/Starter deployments can store up to two backups at a time; Professional deployments can store many, at standard usage pricing. Generating a backup uses database bandwidth to read documents and file bandwidth for user files; storing the backup is billed like user file storage. On the pricing page, file storage is 1 GB included then $0.033/GB on Free & Starter, and 100 GB included then $0.03/GB on Professional. Database I/O is 1 GB included then $0.22/GB on Free & Starter, and 50 GB included then $0.20/GB on Professional. Data egress is 1 GB included then $0.132/GB on Free & Starter, and 50 GB included then $0.12/GB on Professional. The limits doc, as returned in search excerpts, says file storage pricing includes user files and backups, and data egress includes generating and restoring backups. Dedicated D1024 and D2048 deployments also get automatic physical backups every 24 hours, kept up to 7 days, included in dedicated-hardware price, not downloadable, and restorable only through Convex support. — [Convex backup and restore](https://docs.convex.dev/database/backup-restore); [Convex pricing](https://www.convex.dev/pricing); [Convex limits](https://docs.convex.dev/production/state/limits)
- Convex enterprise PDF, printed as current as of 28 September 2026: Business/Enterprise $2,500 monthly minimum. Active dedicated deployments, US East, $2,800/month for D1024 and $5,600/month for D2048 (EU West $3,640 and $7,280). Backup/restore is logical on smaller classes and high-speed physical on D1024/D2048. Premier support is $2,000/month or 7% of usage, whichever is higher. — [Convex enterprise pricing PDF](https://www.convex.dev/enterprise-pricing.pdf)
- Convex has an open feature request, “Point in Time Recovery (PITR, Backups by Second),” submitted 11 June 2026, status Requested, with no development updates. The request text says a weekly backup does not cover enterprise recoverability. Public backing shown: 85 from 8 supporters. — [Convex Ship request #363](https://ship.convex.dev/requests/js7dggash7ddpw301ckdhaggp188fbkr)

### Inferences
- The price a small production app already pays its database host for “some backups” is usually the platform plan, not a backup product: about $25/month on Supabase Pro or Convex Professional (Convex is per developer, so a three-developer team is $75 before usage), $0 platform fee plus history storage on Neon Launch, or $5–$15/month for a tiny PlanetScale Postgres node with backups included. Meaningful point-in-time recovery is the expensive step on Supabase (about $100–$400/month per project) and is simply missing on Convex.
- A hosted Convex backup service is not competing with a $100 PITR add-on Convex does not sell. It is competing with Convex Professional’s included 7-day daily snapshot, with the customer’s own `npx convex export`, and with dedicated hardware whose physical backups the customer cannot download.

### Gaps
- Firestore’s two pricing pages disagree slightly on the backup unit ($0.000041096 per GiB on the Cloud table versus $0.00004 per GiB-hour on the Enterprise page). The PITR retention window for Firestore was not confirmed.
- Realtime Database backup pricing was not retrieved; findings above are Firestore.
- Xata’s current retention window and whether PITR is included in the $0.28/GB storage rate were not on the current pricing page. The older Pro-plan PITR policy should not be quoted as 2026 pricing.
- PlanetScale disk overage rates vary by region; only the backup overage of $0.023/GB-month was confirmed, not a full small-database invoice.
- Convex limits-page rates were seen in search excerpts and match the pricing page, but the limits page itself was not re-fetched in full.

## What do generic backup SaaS products charge, and what is a typical price for a small production database backup with offsite storage?

### Takeaway
A hosted control plane for one or a few small databases, with the option to write to the customer’s own S3-compatible bucket, is priced from a free first job up to about $14–$49 per month. Agency-scale consoles are about $99–$199 per month. Storage on the vendor’s own disk is the metered part; bring-your-own storage is advertised as unlimited on the paid plans checked.

### Cited Findings
- SimpleBackups, observed 8 October 2026. Plan cards: Lite $49/month for 5 included backups, 50 GB included SimpleStorage, schedule up to every 12 hours, unlimited own storage, flexible retention, anomaly detection. PLUS $99/month for 20 included backups, 200 GB SimpleStorage, schedule up to every 1 hour, and “everything in Lite” plus unlimited team members, projects and access control, and a disaster-recovery dashboard. Max $299/month for 100 included backups, 1 TB SimpleStorage, schedule up to every 5 minutes, developer API, white-glove support, custom integrations, and “ISO/SOC2 compliance Automation.” FAQ: the Basic plan is free and allows the first backup job; more jobs are add-ons. Own storage is not quota-limited; SimpleStorage is. Database backup is described as self-hosted or managed MySQL, PostgreSQL, MongoDB, and Redis. The page says “over 3000 users served,” which is a marketing claim, not audited revenue. A lower comparison table on the same fetch did not line up cleanly with the plan cards (it showed included storage figures of 1 GB, 50 GB, and 1 TB against plan columns). The plan cards are the figures used here. Enterprise (SLA, SSO, custom contracts, HIPAA BAAs) is sales-quoted. — [SimpleBackups pricing](https://simplebackups.com/pricing)
- SnapShooter, observed 8 October 2026. The top plan cards, prices excluding VAT: Lite $14/month, up to 3 backup resources, daily backups, daily retention, unlimited bring-your-own storage, 10 GB included storage. Startup $39/month, up to 20 resources, twice a day, daily/weekly/monthly retention, 60 GB included. Business $79/month, up to 50 resources, every 5 minutes, 120 GB included, unlimited team members, encryption. Agency $199/month, up to 250 resources, every 5 minutes, 240 GB included, encryption. Agency Pro $1,400/month, up to 10,000 resources, 10 TB included. A free-looking block on the same page lists 1 backup job, daily retention, and 500 MB included storage, without a clear plan name in the fetch. Later sections of the same page show Lite at $13/month and Business at $39/month, which conflicts with the top cards; the top cards are treated as the monthly list, and the lower figures may be annual or a scrape mix. Storage targets listed include Amazon S3, DigitalOcean Spaces, Wasabi, Backblaze B2, Firebase, and custom S3. 14-day refund. — [SnapShooter pricing](https://snapshooter.com/pricing)
- BackupSheep’s pricing page says the product available now is a self-hosted beta under GNU GPLv3, with no BackupSheep cloud in the data path. It says a managed edition may follow and is not currently available. A third-party comparison site (Spotsaas) lists BackupSheep “starting at $25/month” and SimpleBackups “starting at $5/month.” Those starting prices contradict BackupSheep’s own page and SimpleBackups’ own plan cards, so they are not used as prices. — [BackupSheep pricing](https://backupsheep.com/pricing/); [Spotsaas comparison](https://www.spotsaas.com/compare/simplebackups-vs-backupsheep)
- A 2026 comparison article says SnapShooter schedules MySQL, PostgreSQL, and MongoDB backups plus server and DigitalOcean snapshots, can write to the customer’s S3-compatible bucket or included storage, and that DigitalOcean acquired SnapShooter in January 2023 after a 2017 founding. That acquisition date is from the comparison article, not from a DigitalOcean announcement fetched in this pass. — [SimpleBackups vs SnapShooter (faun.dev)](https://faun.dev/toolbox/simplebackups-vs-snapshooter/)
- For a platform-bundled small database, offsite-or-included backup cost on top of the node is often near zero until retention or PITR is extended. PlanetScale’s smallest single-node Postgres is $5/month with default backups included (2× disk, then $0.023/GB-month). Neon Launch has no monthly minimum; history is $0.20/GB-month only for retained WAL on root branches. Supabase’s documented full-month 7-day PITR stack is $130 after credits, versus $25 Pro with 7-day daily backups and no PITR. — [PlanetScale Postgres pricing](https://planetscale.com/docs/postgres/pricing); [Neon plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans); [Supabase PITR usage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/point-in-time-recovery)

### Inferences
- The going rate for “someone else runs a daily backup of a small database into a bucket” is about $0–$14 for a single job (SimpleBackups’ free first job, or SnapShooter Lite at $14 for up to three resources with 10 GB of their storage and unlimited customer storage) and about $49 when the product is sold as a small bundle of jobs with vendor storage (SimpleBackups Lite). Paying $99–$299 is for more jobs, faster schedules, team access, or compliance exports, not for one small database.
- Generic hosts do not list Convex. Their database connectors are MySQL, PostgreSQL, MongoDB, and Redis. A Convex customer cannot buy SnapShooter or SimpleBackups as a drop-in.

### Gaps
- Add-on prices for extra SimpleBackups jobs and extra SimpleStorage gigabytes are mentioned but not numbered on the fetched page, except a Tadabase objects add-on of $10/month for +20 tables, which is not a database-backup price.
- SnapShooter’s conflicting $13 and $39 figures were not resolved by a second fetch.
- No invoice example was found for “one 1 GB production database, daily, 30-day retention, customer-owned R2” from these vendors. The plan minimums above are the closest public prices.

## What public evidence is there that someone will pay?

### Takeaway
People pay the database vendor for backups inside a broader plan, and they pay generic backup hosts on published monthly plans. Evidence that they will pay a third party for Convex backups specifically is thin: the only Convex-specific public signal found is a small feature request asking Convex itself for point-in-time recovery. The closest willingness-to-pay pattern is Supabase users being told to build their own off-site copy, plus a Supabase-specific tool offering a free tier for one project.

### Cited Findings
- Platform prices above are themselves willingness-to-pay evidence: Supabase sells 7-day PITR at about $100/month per project on top of Pro; Turso sells longer restore windows inside $5.99, $29, and $499 plans; Convex gates scheduled backups behind $25 per developer per month. — [Supabase Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing); [Turso pricing](https://turso.tech/pricing.md); [Convex pricing](https://www.convex.dev/pricing)
- Supabase’s own docs tell free projects to dump and keep off-site copies. Third-party guides repeat that the Free plan has no automated backups (Backup Drill dates the Pro $25 / 7-day daily backup description “as of August 2026”). VaultKeep’s blog says Free projects have no automated backups, Pro projects get seven days inside Supabase, and a copy you can prove restores has to be made by the customer. The same post says VaultKeep is free for one project and adds storage sync, retention, missed-window alerts, verification, and one-click restore into a fresh Supabase project. That is product marketing, not a paid price. — [Supabase backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups); [Backup Drill](https://backupdrill.com/guides/supabase-free-plan-backups); [VaultKeep blog](https://vaultkeep.dev/blog/supabase-free-tier-backups-github-actions/)
- SimpleBackups publishes paid plans from $49 to $299 per month and claims “over 3000 users served.” One testimonial on that page says the customer switched from CloudBack because one product covered databases and GitHub repos. No revenue, churn, or average revenue per account was published. — [SimpleBackups pricing](https://simplebackups.com/pricing)
- Convex Ship request #363 (11 June 2026) asks Convex for second-level PITR for enterprise customers. Shown support is 85 backing points from 8 supporters, with no status change. That is a request for the platform to build the feature, not a price someone offered a third party. — [Convex Ship request #363](https://ship.convex.dev/requests/js7dggash7ddpw301ckdhaggp188fbkr)
- Shopify’s app listing for Rewind Backups shows Backup + Restore from $19/month (up to 300 orders/month, 365-day history, 7-day trial) and Protection Suite from $99/month, rating 4.3 from 629 reviews, launched 5 January 2016. Rewind’s own pricing page, for other platforms, says Standard starts at $4.00 per user per month and Advanced at $5.20 per user per month, with the calculator covering platforms such as Jira, GitHub, and monday.com; Advanced adds configurable retention, bring-your-own key, bring-your-own storage, and HIPAA and DORA compliance. A Rewind help article updated 27 October 2025 describes a Shopify price increase (example: $99/month moving to $119/month). — [Shopify app listing](https://apps.shopify.com/backup); [Rewind pricing](https://rewind.com/pricing/); [Rewind price-update article](https://help.rewind.com/hc/en-us/articles/41129110623899-Price-update-for-Rewind-Backups-for-Shopify-and-Protection-Suite)
- G2 and Capterra were not used. No review-sentiment sample was collected.

### Inferences
- The buyer most likely to pay for a hosted Convex backup service is an agency (or a freelancer with a book of client deployments), not a solo developer and not a company shopping for auditor-ready restore evidence.
- Solo developers are the weakest incremental buyers. Convex Professional already includes daily backups at $25 per developer, which is inside the same band as SnapShooter Lite ($14) and below SimpleBackups Lite ($49). Free and Starter users can take two manual backups and can export with the CLI. Supabase’s analogue of this user is served by free GitHub Actions recipes, not by a $49 host.
- Agencies match the product shape the founder described: one operator, many client deployments, backups landing in customer R2/S3 or in operator storage. SimpleBackups prices that console (projects, access control, disaster-recovery dashboard) at $99/month for 20 jobs. SnapShooter prices an Agency plan at $199/month for 250 resources. Those are fleet prices, not per-database prices.
- Companies that want restore evidence do pay, but they pay the platform’s compliance tier or a backup vendor’s top tier: Supabase Team from $599/month, Convex Business from $2,500/month, SimpleBackups Max at $299/month with compliance automation, Rewind Advanced with HIPAA/DORA and bring-your-own storage. That buyer will ask for the backup operator’s own SOC 2, a BAA, and retention they can show an auditor. Convex Professional already includes Convex’s HIPAA/SOC 2 reports; it does not include a customer-held archive or a restore drill report. A small Convex-only host can sell the missing artifact, and it will lose deals where the auditor requires the vendor’s compliance program. SimpleBackups hides that paperwork on Enterprise.

### Gaps
- No Indie Hackers or public revenue post with cited dollars was found for a database-backup or Convex-backup product.
- VaultKeep’s paid price, if any, was not on the page retrieved. “Free for one project” is the only figure.
- No public count of paying Convex production teams, agencies using Convex, or Professional subscribers was found. The pricing page shows “GitHub 21,437 stars” and “npm 2M weekly.” Those are not customers and are not an addressable-set estimate.
- No G2 or Capterra sentiment was collected.

## Is a Convex-only backup product too small a niche, or is specificity an advantage? Which single-database backup products stayed narrow, and which expanded?

### Takeaway
Specificity is required, because the generic backup hosts checked do not support Convex, and it is also a ceiling, because the only public Convex demand signal found is eight supporters of a platform feature request. The closest single-platform backup companies did not stay single-platform: Rewind now prices many SaaS apps, and SnapShooter’s catalog is multi-engine and multi-cloud. BackupSheep is the nearer open-source twin, and it still has no hosted product.

### Cited Findings
- SimpleBackups lists database backup for MySQL, PostgreSQL, MongoDB, and Redis. SnapShooter’s pricing page is built around backup resources, DigitalOcean snapshots, and S3-compatible destinations, not a Convex connector. Convex’s export format, from its own docs, is a ZIP of per-table `documents.jsonl` plus optional `_storage` files, produced by the dashboard or `npx convex export`. That is not a Postgres or Mongo dump. — [SimpleBackups pricing](https://simplebackups.com/pricing); [SnapShooter pricing](https://snapshooter.com/pricing); [Convex backup and restore](https://docs.convex.dev/database/backup-restore)
- Rewind’s Shopify app launched 5 January 2016 and still sells Shopify backup from $19/month. The current Rewind pricing page prices Standard and Advanced per user for platforms including Jira, GitHub, and monday.com. So the company sells backups for more than one platform. A founding narrative beyond the Shopify launch date was not fetched from a primary history page. — [Shopify app listing](https://apps.shopify.com/backup); [Rewind pricing](https://rewind.com/pricing/)
- SnapShooter still sells an Agency plan at $199/month and lists many storage providers. The faun.dev comparison, a secondary source, says it began in 2017 and was acquired by DigitalOcean in January 2023, and describes it as backups for several database engines plus servers and DigitalOcean snapshots. — [SnapShooter pricing](https://snapshooter.com/pricing); [faun.dev comparison](https://faun.dev/toolbox/simplebackups-vs-snapshooter/)
- BackupSheep is open source, self-hosted, and multi-source (websites, databases, servers, volumes, cloud). The operator says a managed edition is not available now and may come later. — [BackupSheep pricing](https://backupsheep.com/pricing/)
- VaultKeep’s blog positions the product as Supabase backup and restore, free for one project, beside a do-it-yourself GitHub Actions path. No evidence was found that it has expanded past Supabase, and no paid plan page was retrieved. — [VaultKeep blog](https://vaultkeep.dev/blog/supabase-free-tier-backups-github-actions/)
- Inputs that bound a Convex-only set, without turning them into a market size: Convex’s pricing page shows 21,437 GitHub stars and 2 million weekly npm downloads (8 October 2026). The PITR request has 8 supporters (11 June 2026). Professional allows up to 300 deployments per team; Free & Starter allows 40. None of these is a count of teams that would pay for off-site backups. — [Convex pricing](https://www.convex.dev/pricing); [Convex Ship request #363](https://ship.convex.dev/requests/js7dggash7ddpw301ckdhaggp188fbkr)

### Inferences
- A Convex-only hosted service is not “a smaller SimpleBackups.” Generic tools cannot perform the backup, so specificity is the product. The same specificity means the host should not expect Rewind-like expansion unless it later adds other BaaS exports (Supabase, Firebase, Turso). Rewind and SnapShooter grew by adding platforms and engines, not by remaining the backup product for one database.
- Keeping the open-source self-hosted app matches BackupSheep’s current posture and the Supabase do-it-yourself guides. Those users are visible and mostly unpaid. The hosted offer has to be the agency console and the off-site copy, which the self-hosted app does not operate for a customer.
- Eight supporters on a platform feature request is not evidence of a large niche. It is evidence that second-level recovery is asked for rarely in public, and that those people asked Convex rather than a vendor.

### Gaps
- No commercial Convex-only backup host was found, so there is no direct competitor price or post-mortem of a failed Convex-only product.
- SnapShooter’s acquisition was not confirmed on a DigitalOcean primary page in this pass.
- VaultKeep’s later product scope and price were not established.

## What do agencies typically pay for client backup and disaster recovery, when a public package exists?

### Takeaway
Public agency backup packages cluster around $49–$199 per month for a multi-client console, with storage either included up to a few hundred gigabytes or billed to the customer’s own cloud. Broader website-care retainers that happen to include backups are priced per site, about $49–$97, and one vendor claims agencies resell care at $99–$499 per client. Those care plans are not database-only products.

### Cited Findings
- SnapShooter Agency is $199/month for up to 250 backup resources, backups every 5 minutes, daily/weekly/monthly retention, unlimited bring-your-own storage, 240 GB included storage, unlimited team members, and encryption. Agency Pro is $1,400/month for up to 10,000 resources and 10 TB included. Prices exclude VAT. Observed 8 October 2026. — [SnapShooter pricing](https://snapshooter.com/pricing)
- SimpleBackups PLUS is $99/month for 20 backup jobs, 200 GB SimpleStorage, hourly schedules, unlimited team members, projects and access control, and a disaster-recovery dashboard. The plan is labeled for “SaaS, Agencies and SMEs.” Max at $299/month adds the compliance automation and a 5-minute schedule. — [SimpleBackups pricing](https://simplebackups.com/pricing)
- DailyBkup Agency is $49 per month: one server license for up to 50 domains, multi-server fleet management, a multi-client workspace, white-label analytical reports, role-based access, and priority support. Storage is bring-your-own-cloud and is billed by the customer’s cloud provider, not by DailyBkup. This is a website/server backup product, not a Convex or Postgres host. — [DailyBkup pricing](https://dailybkup.com/pricing)
- remote-backups.com prices agency off-site storage at €8.50 per TB per month, with per-client datastores, and states a typical agency cost of €17–€43/month for 2–5 TB across 10–40 clients. ### Inferences
- An agency-mode Convex host should be packaged as one monthly console, not as a per-deployment tax that exceeds Convex Professional. The comparable package prices are $49 (DailyBkup, many sites, customer-owned storage), $99 (SimpleBackups, 20 jobs plus a disaster-recovery dashboard), and $199 (SnapShooter, 250 resources). A per-client restore-evidence report can sit inside that console; the WordPress care market shows agencies already resell “backups included” inside a larger retainer, so the agency is the payer and the client often never sees a backup invoice.
- Restore-evidence companies are a second, smaller payer. They are the SimpleBackups Max / Rewind Advanced buyer, at a few hundred dollars per month per organization, and they are more likely to buy Convex Business ($2,500 minimum) or Supabase Team ($599) first. A Convex-only host can sell them longer retention and a drill report only if it does not pretend to replace the platform contract.

### Gaps
- No public rate card was found for an agency retainer that is specifically “Convex backup” or “BaaS disaster recovery.”
- The $99–$499 care range is one vendor’s claim about its customers, not a census of agencies.
- SecRealm prices are CAD; they were not converted.
